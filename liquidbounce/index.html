LiquidBounce.net is temporarily offline

We are currently dealing with an abuse report from Mojang. As a precaution, we have taken liquidbounce.net offline while we investigate.

Until this is resolved, our website won't be accessible.

Updated 17 October 2026

Why we think the claim is baseless

The notice names only our homepage as the infringing URL and minecraft.net as a whole as the original work. It points to no file, page, image or line of code, and its field for evidence is empty. liquidbounce.net does not host or distribute any content, files or software from Minecraft or Mojang.

It was filed on behalf of Microsoft by Tracer, a brand protection company that uses AI agents to detect and take down alleged infringements. We are not the first project to get a notice like this from them. This year, Tracer got the indie game Allumeria pulled from Steam and the open-source game engine Luanti pulled from Google Play, which had already received a similar notice from Tracer in 2023. Neither notice named any specific infringing material, and Microsoft later withdrew both.

Luanti’s Android app is currently not available on the Google Play Store due to a baseless DMCA notice filed on behalf of Microsoft by Tracer.AI, alleging that Luanti infringes Minecraft’s copyright. The Luanti app does not contain any proprietary code or assets, from Minecraft or otherwise.

If a company can repeatedly submit the same DMCA notice with no explanation or evidence, taking projects down for months at a time, the system is broken.

We call on Microsoft, Mojang, and Tracer.AI to stop relying on AI tools to send inaccurate and vague DMCA notices. Detection must be verified by a human and, more importantly, substantiated with tangible proof.

The Luanti team, Luanti removed from Google Play due to baseless AI copyright notice

Microsoft's copyright claim cites a screenshot from Allumeria […] alongside one from Minecraft without any specific similarities beyond using voxels.

Valve will automatically take a game off Steam if a DMCA is issued against it, so this all could just be a bunch of robots reacting with little to no actual humans examining the circumstances.

PC Gamer, Microsoft has revoked the DMCA takedown that forced an indie Minecraft-like off Steam

Why the website stays offline anyway

We think this claim is baseless, and we could probably just keep hosting our website. In practice, we can't. Cloudflare passes every complaint it receives straight on to our hosting provider, and Tracer can send yet another automated notice at any time and take our servers down again.

OVHcloud had already suspended our server for "Hacked", which caused a disruption in our services. We are moving our websites to a new hoster, but even that is too high a risk.

A small team can't fight notices like this the way a large corporation can file them. The developer of Allumeria described the same position:

The safest thing to do is just roll over and die basically. Allow them to delete the game. If I do fight it, then i could be ruined by a large corporation.

Unomelon, developer of Allumeria, on the Allumeria Discord

liquidbounce.net stays offline until the complaint is withdrawn or Tracer names specific material that we can review.
